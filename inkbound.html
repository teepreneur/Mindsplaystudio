Inkbound

Write the story back.

Once upon a page

A book that needs its words back.

You are the Scribe, a small hero with a big quill. The Eraser has crept into your storybook and rubbed out bridges, colours and even time. Golden words float above the page. Catch one, write it on the world, and the world becomes what the word says.

  1. Catch a golden wordWords like “frozen”, “tiny” and “grow” float over the hills. Walk into one and it flies into your quill.
  2. Find something to write onAn ink river you can’t cross. A seed by a tear in the page. A pile of fallen books.
  3. Choose your wordWhich adjective will you write on the river? Should the seed grow in the past, now, or in the future?
  4. Watch the story changeThe river freezes, the seed shoots up into a tree bridge, and the page fills with colour again.
The chapters

Every chapter, a new kind of word.

Each chapter is a page of the book with its own puzzles and its own kind of word to master. Restore the page and it is written back into the story.

    What makes it different

    Grammar you can stand on.

    In Inkbound, words are not answers in a box. They are tools that change the world, so children see what a word means by what it does.

    Words do what they say

    Write “frozen” on the ink river and it turns to ice you can walk on. Write “tiny” and it shrinks to a trickle you can step over.

    Time is a tool

    Verbs come in the past, the present and the future. A seed that “will grow” becomes a tree. Books that “stood” remember how to be stairs.

    Wrong words are funny, not failures

    Write “grows” on the seed and you get a tiny wiggling sprout. The story tells you why, and you try again. Nobody loses a life.

    Reading is how you win

    The narrator’s lines and the quest notes hold the clues. Children read for meaning because the next move depends on it.

    The name comes after the fun

    “You caught ‘grow’! It’s a verb: a doing word.” The grammar word is taught at the moment it is useful, in plain language.

    A paper world to explore

    Walk, jump and climb across pop-up villages, orchards and book stairs on a desk of giant books. Every chapter ends with a recap of the words you used.

    Try a page

    Before the ink dries

    The Eraser has smudged a word in each sentence. Pick the right word before the ink dries.

    1. Read the sentence and look for clues like “yesterday” or “tomorrow”.
    2. Tap a word, or press 1, 2 or 3.
    3. Faster answers earn more points. Eight sentences a round.
