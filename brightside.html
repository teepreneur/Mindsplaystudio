About the game

An open town full of problems worth fixing.

Brightside is a sunny, colourful riverside town. Walk its streets, talk to neighbours and find out what's broken. Every fix is a hands-on design sandbox with real physics and maths under the hood, and when you get it right, the town visibly changes.

Missions · how a fix works

Broken. Blueprint. Tested. Fixed.

Each mission follows the engineering design process. A neighbour asks for help, you find three field clues, design at the blueprint table within a budget, test it and improve it until it works. Then it gets built in the world.

  1. Ask
  2. Imagine
  3. Plan and create
  4. Test
  5. Improve
  6. Share

    Finale: with the bridge, the court and the garden fixed, the whole town throws a block party in the plaza.

    What makes it different

    No worksheets. Real engineering.

    Brightside asks teens to do what engineers do: look at a real problem, measure it, design, test and try again.

    Field clues

    Measure before you design.

    Twelve metres to cross, thirty students and a bread cart. You collect the facts on site before you touch the blueprint.

    Real physics

    A failed test is data.

    A truss solver works out the force in every beam. Overloaded parts glow red and the game tells you why they failed.

    The crew

    Three friends, three specialities.

    Switch between Kofi, Mei and Ravi at any time. The other two follow you, cheer and chip in with ideas.

    Community Vibe

    The town changes.

    Every fix raises the Community Vibe meter. More neighbours come out, and the plaza gets livelier.

    Career cards

    Meet the real job.

    Each mission ends with a career card: what the engineer does, a real project somewhere in the world, and the subjects that lead there.

    Open world

    Explore at your own pace.

    Roam the streets, chat to neighbours and hunt for 20 hidden Idea Sparks, each one a short engineering fact.

    Mini-game

    Lights Out

    The court lights are dead and the evening league is cancelled. Power four floodlights with the sun, from 6 pm to 10 pm, for three days. Day 2 is cloudy. The budget is $1,100.

    1. Pick the lamps, the number of solar panels and the batteries.
    2. Check the notebook: energy in has to beat energy out.
    3. Press Test and watch the battery over three days.
    4. If the lights cut out, change your design and test again. Spend $900 or less for three stars.

    This is a small version of the Lights Out mission. In the game you also wire the panels in series or parallel and watch the voltage.
